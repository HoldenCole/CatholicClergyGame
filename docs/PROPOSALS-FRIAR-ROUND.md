# The friar's round: a Dominican life played end to end, what broke, and twenty proposals

A companion to `PROPOSALS-DIOCESAN-ROUND.md`, for the religious campaign (E3). One Dominican of the Province of St. Joseph, seed `friar-life-1`, Daniel Reyes, in religion Albert, played from the novitiate at 22 to the end through the engine (an active harness: the hours set each week, an office of the house asked for, letters to the provincial, permissions, a petition for a foundation, every ask said yes to, every election accepted), then the snapshots walked in the browser at 1280×800.

## 2. Fixed in this round

- **A sitting provincial was elected prior of his house, and the record wrote "he left the office of provincial before its term, for the office of prior".** Twice in one life. The provincial, and the province's provincial NPC, no longer stand for prior.
- **A friar teaching in Rome was elected prior and provincial at home and governed from the Aventine.** A man away (Rome, the generalate, the Holy See, any posting away) neither votes in his house's chapter nor can be elected by it.
- **A prior sent to another house kept the priorship of the house he left** until the next election. The chair stays with the house: the transfer vacates it, "sent to another house".
- **Direction never ended.** Three Manchester priests he began directing at 30 were still on his list at 70, forty years and four dioceses later. A move out of the diocese ends every direction, with one note and a name for each man.
- **The formation sheet named the novitiate** (St. Joseph Priory) for a student living at the house of studies. It names the house he is in.
- **The plate called him "Daniel Reyes, Friar, in formation work".** It reads "Fr. Albert Reyes, O.P., Prior": the religious name he was given, the title his vows allow, the order's letters.
- **The cloister's door plates read "Rector", "Director", "Room 12"** from the diocesan seminary. A friar's corridor reads Library, Confessional (or Master, for a student), Cell, Prior, Common, and the mat says "To the city".
- **The friar's sheets were the game's longest**: House 7.8–9.7 screens, Week 4.3–6.7. The week no longer carries the whole record (the Record tab does); the house folds the men, what is said, the order's character, the prior's offices, building and purse, the permissions, the offices on offer, the houses of the province, and the seminary's men. House is now 3–6 screens depending on the desk, Week under 2.

## 3. Quality of life (Q1–Q10)

**Q1. The circle for a friar.** The Circle sheet groups people as family, class, brother priests, chancery, bishops, parish: the men he lives with land under "Others", and after 41 years in the order "0 of them friends". Add groups the friar's life is made of: the house (the men at table now), the novitiate class, the province (priors, the provincial, the council), directees, the bishops of the dioceses he has lived in. The "have a word" and "make amends" buttons should work on brothers.

**Q2. The ending shelf for a friar.** It lists "Fr. Mark McCabe, parishioner, dead" for a brother of the house and "You were appointed 17 times" for obediences. The shelf should read a friar's life: the houses he lived in and for how long, the offices and terms, the foundation and what became of it, the men he formed as novice master, the brothers who buried him.

**Q3. The provincial's desk.** As provincial for three terms the player had nothing to do: no sheet, no acts. The province ran fifteen million dollars into the red, "the house ran short and the province made it up, and the council noticed" every year, hostility at 75, a house that must close. Give the provincial a desk like the prior's and the bishop's: assignments to sign, a house to close, the finances, a visitation of the houses.

**Q4. The consultation should say what the house needs of him.** The three options read "they badly need a man; you could do it; and a young friar should see it" for every house. Say what the work is (the parish's Spanish Mass, the school's theology chair), what the house is like against his own observance, and who is there that he knows.

**Q5. The rumours in the friar's idiom.** "The bishop has heard what is being said: Fr. Reyes is drinking" and "Fr. Reyes is going up; the letter came from the chancery this month" are the diocesan lines. For a friar the one who hears should be the prior or the provincial, and the letter should come from the provincial's council.

**Q6. The general's years.** Nine years as Master of the Order fired thirteen scenes. The general chapter that elected him is a letter; the visitations, the provinces against him, the dicastery, the pope, the last chapter are a scene each. Make the term a posting with a week of its own (the room in Rome, the provinces on the desk, the letters to the order) and a digest that reports the order, not the home house.

**Q7. The years in Rome.** The Roman faculty fired its seven scenes in the first year and then nothing for four. The Rome week (thesis, Italian, the chapel, calcio) is the diocesan student's; a friar teaching at the Angelicum should have the faculty's week and the house on the Aventine's horarium.

**Q8. Strain that does something.** Strain sat at 100 for the last six years of the life and the only consequence was a line. For a friar there is no sick week, no sabbatical offered, no infirmarian. At 80 the infirmarian should speak, the prior should cut the week, and past 65 the province should offer the infirmary house or a sabbatical, with the death roll reading strain.

**Q9. The foundation's failure should be a scene, not a flag.** His house in Baltimore failed at two and a half years "because the vocations never came and the men were needed elsewhere" while he was away as provincial. He learned of it from a flag. The provincial's letter closing the house, the last Mass, the men reassigned, and the bishop's reaction deserve a scene, and the founder's regard should move.

**Q10. The year in review for a friar.** The review reads "the house: devoted, about the same. The province: devoted. The provincial: devoted, warmer. The bishop: warm." Every constituency reads "devoted" after year 15 because standing saturates at 100. Add the horarium kept, the reputation grown, the house's cohesion and money, the men who entered and left, and the obedience accepted or refused that year.

## 4. Depth (D1–D10)

**D1. Brothers who become friends.** In 41 years no friar rose above 40 regard; the two men he was closest to at the end were at 40 and 37. A diocesan priest in the same seed had a dozen men at 60–100. The house should build regard the way a rectory does: the table, choir, recreation, the hours given to the community, a shared posting. Ten years in a house of eight men should leave one or two friends and one enemy. The Augustinian friendship slots are the model; the Dominican should get the same in a looser form.

**D2. The chapters that keep electing him.** Twelve elections in 41 years: prior seven times, provincial three, general once, novice master besides. Perceived ambition sat at 0 the whole life. Term limits stop consecutive terms, not the alternation prior-provincial-prior. Add a rest between offices (a term in the ranks), a cost to age (a man of 70 elected prior against the "generation in their seventies who run everything" complication should be the province's problem, named), and a "the chapter turns elsewhere" chance that rises with every term served.

**D3. The formation years as a novitiate.** Four or fewer scenes a year for seven years: the seminary draws only on three played weeks a year, and the friar's formation pool of sixty once-only scenes never gets through. The novitiate is the most formative year of the life and it is nearly silent. Draw formation scenes weekly the way a posting does, at a lower chance, and add the novice master's talks, the first Christmas away, the clothing, the vote before profession as scenes.

**D4. Cohesion and observance with teeth.** The houses sat at cohesion 45–59 and observance 34–67 for 41 years; his own observance drifted from 65 to 35 while he was prior and nothing happened. Low cohesion should cost the house men (a departure), observance drift should bring the provincial's visitation, and the prior's rule should be felt by the men (a house moved rubs, the design says; make it rub).

**D5. The province's money and the closures.** Balance −$15,099,305, trajectory shrinking, retirement burden 0.27, four "foundation:invited" flags from bishops and a "house that must close" scene: the province is dying and the game has one scene for it. Make the province's finances a yearly letter the provincial answers (close a house, sell a building, refuse a foundation, ask the general), with the trajectory moving.

**D6. Obedience with a cost.** Five assignments in 41 years, every one "good", and a letter to the provincial answered yes four times out of five. Add reluctance and refusal with consequences that last (a man who refused an obedience is not sent again; a man who asked twice is "the one who writes"), and make the provincial's letter sometimes send him where he asked not to go.

**D7. The bishop's file across dioceses.** The friar left Manchester at 67 regard with its bishop, Baltimore at 63, New York at 70, and each diocese remembered him ("Back in Washington after 14 years: the same bishop, who remembers you a little"). Make the file do more: a bishop who remembers him asks the provincial for him by name; a bishop who does not refuses the order's parish; the nuncio reads the files when a mitre is discussed.

**D8. The novice master's men.** As novice master for four years he formed nobody the game names. The diocesan game names the summer seminarians a pastor formed and follows them for life (D5 of the diocesan round). The men a novice master clothed and the students a master of students taught should be his for life: their professions, their ordinations, their leaving, and the chapter where they vote for him.

**D9. Reputations that overshoot and are tested.** Confessor, professor, and spiritual director all reached 100 and stayed; "the formator" was his identity for 15 years and nothing asked anything of it. The design (§8.3) says a reputation not earned is a liability: the game should ask him to do the thing in public (a retreat for the province's priests, a book, a penitent who matters) and let it go badly.

**D10. The cardinal and the friar.** Made a cardinal at 61 while provincial, he was elected prior of a priory two years later and served, and the record never mentioned the red again. A cardinal of the order (real ones exist) should leave the ranks: consistories and a titular church in Rome, the general asking his help with the dicastery, the province proud and a little embarrassed, and the chapters electing without him.

## 5. Shipped since

- **Batch A (Q1–Q5).** The friar's circle; the shelf that reads a friar's life; the provincial's desk; consultations that say what the house needs; talk in the friar's idiom.
- **Batch B (Q6–Q10).** The general's years, the faculty's, and the Curia's for a friar are repeatable scenes drawn at a Roman posting's own rate, and the general's review counts the order; past the sick line the infirmarian or the prior cuts the week, a sabbatical or the infirmary house takes a man off the work for its weeks, the province offers past sixty-five, and the death roll reads strain; a house closed while its founder was away is a scene; the year in review has the friar's rows (the common life kept, what he is known for, the house and its men and purse, the obedience of the year, the province's books for a provincial).
- **Batch C (D1, D4, D3, D9).** The men at table build regard by the common life kept (the table, the choir, the hours given to the community, the house's cohesion) and by an affinity of the two men rolled once from the seed; every year at the same table is a bond, a quarrel at chapter in a house at odds with itself is one too, and ten years leave a friend or two and an enemy. A cold house loses men (leaving is likelier, a professed man asks to be moved), drifted observance brings the provincial's visitation, the prior's rule rubs the men of the other mind, and three scenes read the flags. A friar's formation draws a scene at a low chance every week, not on three played weeks a year, and the novitiate has eight scenes of its own. Six reputations are tested in public (the province's retreat, the book reviewed, the penitent who matters, the formators directed, the broken house, the death the town watches): the thing done well needs the stats behind it, and otherwise the reputation shrinks to the size of the man.
- **Batch D (the offices deepened, D8).** The men a novice master clothes and a master of students teaches are his for life: which offices form men is data on the order (`forms` on the office), the year makes the house's novices or students his (warmer, a bond the years keep, a tag the electorate reads and votes warmer for), the record follows each to simple vows, solemn vows, ordination, leaving, or death, the House sheet lists them under "Your men", the shelf under "The men you formed", and the scenes reach them by `@formed_man`. Before this round no scene read a house office; now the procurator, sacristan, infirmarian, guest master, librarian, cantor, local promoter of vocations, and the lay chapter's assistant each have their own (the roofer's bill, the receipts, the auditor, Holy Week, the chalice in the safe, the infirmarian's night, the count, the bishop's secretary, a bed for the night, the librarian's boxes, the pitch, the boy at the door, the second Tuesday), and the novice master, master of students, regent, promoter of preaching, vocation director, prior, and provincial have new ones of theirs.
